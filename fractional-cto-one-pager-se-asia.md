## The gap

Post-seed companies sit in a bind: they need senior technical leadership well
before they can afford it full-time, and the board is asking for an AI
strategy that no one on the team is senior enough to own. Those are usually
two hires. This is one seat that covers both.

## What an engagement looks like

One to two days a week, embedded with the team:

- **Architecture and technical direction** — the decisions that are expensive
  to reverse, made with someone who has made them before.
- **Team and hiring** — leveling the engineering org, closing senior
  candidates, building the process the next stage needs.
- **AI strategy that ships** — owned inside the company, not outsourced to a
  deck.

And the part a normal fractional CTO doesn't bring: I deploy my own
open-source agent infrastructure into the company. A **team brain** — shared
memory grown from your team's actual knowledge, searchable by meaning,
answering in context, running on your own servers.

**It's yours when I leave.** The standard failure mode of fractional
leadership is that the context walks out the door with the exec. This
engagement leaves it behind, running: an agent that holds what the team
learned, owned by the company, open source top to bottom.

## Two ways to engage

**The sprint — 4–6 weeks, fixed scope.** A technical audit at due-diligence
depth, plus a team brain deployed and seeded with the company's context.
Bounded, priced up front, useful on its own — and the fastest way to find out
whether the ongoing engagement fits.

**Fractional CTO — ongoing.** One to two days a week, everything above.
Typically follows the sprint.
