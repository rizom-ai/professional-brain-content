## What this project is

Institutions are shifting memory and judgment to AI at organisational scale — retrieval instead of remembering, fluent synthesis instead of deliberation. The individual effects are studied. The institutional effects are not, though that is where consequential decisions are made. **What happens to a society when its organisations can retrieve everything and remember nothing?**

This project builds and runs a public instrument for asking that question, and publishes what the instrument yields.

The instrument is an open, provenance-tracked research notebook: a self-hosted, plain-text knowledge base where the reading, the working distinctions, the revisions and the dead ends are all visible, and every claim in every published essay traces back to what it came from. It runs live for the full twelve months. A reader does not receive conclusions; they watch an argument being made and can check it at any point.

That inversion is the work. Normally the finished text is the publication and the thinking is discarded. Here the thinking is the publication and the text is its yield.

And the instrument is an argument. A project about institutions that can no longer say why they decided what they decided, conducted inside a memory that can say exactly that, about itself. The critique and its counter-example are the same object.

Three lines of inquiry run through it, continuing the *New Institutions* series (three essays published, including *Hiding in Plain Sight* on the difference between storage and memory):

1. **Retrieval versus memory.** Institutional memory is not storage; it is the capacity to make the past available to present judgment. What exactly is lost when AI summarisation substitutes for it — and can the loss be made observable?
2. **Fluency versus judgment.** AI compresses weak institutional context into confident output. When does that fluency displace the deliberation it imitates?
3. **Cognitive monoculture.** When thousands of organisations route their sense-making through identical foundation models, do their judgments converge — and what does a monoculture of institutional cognition do to public life?

## Why this belongs in Digitale cultuur

The artefact is a working piece of internet culture: a self-hosted, openly licensed instrument that makes a research process public and verifiable. It sits with creative coding and internetcultuur in art. 1.22, and carries the *"onderzoek, experiment en reflectie"* the field definition attaches to those terrains.

It treats AI not as a tool to adopt or refuse but as a cultural force reorganising how institutions remember — and it answers it by building rather than only by arguing. The maker deploys this infrastructure in real organisations, so the inquiry is conducted from inside the practice it examines, not about it from outside.

Everything produced is openly licensed and publicly accessible throughout, which is the condition in art. 6.1.k and also the thesis.

## Deliverables (12 months)

- **The public research notebook** — live from month one, self-hosted, plain text, openly licensed, running for the full period and archived in a citable, durable form at the end. The primary deliverable.
- **Six essays** in the *New Institutions* series, published openly and syndicated, each traceable to the notebook.
- **A flagship long-form publication**, *The Outsourced Institution* — the framework, case evidence from real deployments, and a practical diagnostic: *is this system extending our judgment or replacing it?*
- **The diagnostic as a free-standing open document**, usable by an organisation without reading the book.
- **Two public working sessions** (NL) where the diagnostic is applied live to organisations in the room.

## Method

Practice-based inquiry. As a philosopher-builder who deploys knowledge infrastructure in organisations, I trace what concretely changes when an organisation introduces AI into its knowledge work: which records become retrievable, which workflows reorganise, which outputs get treated as authoritative, who is still expected to remember the reasons.

Transactive memory theory (Wegner) and distributed cognition (Hutchins) are the conceptual frame. The working distinctions — storage versus memory, fluency versus judgment — are tested against real episodes, in public, in the notebook.

## Communicatieplan

Communication is not a phase at the end. The research publishes as it happens, and that is the argument the project makes.

**The notebook is the primary channel** — public from week one, so the work is continuously visible rather than announced on completion.

**Six essays** publish openly at yeehaa.io across the twelve months, syndicated to where the field already reads: the Dutch digital-culture and civic-tech community, and the international network-cultures readership the *New Institutions* series already reaches.

**The flagship publication** is released open access, with the diagnostic separately downloadable so it can be used without the book.

**Two public working sessions** in the Netherlands, structured as working sessions rather than lectures — the diagnostic applied live to organisations present.

**Everything openly licensed** and archived in a form that outlives the project.
