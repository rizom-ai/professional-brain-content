## What this project is

A research and writing project about the memory of digital culture itself: what happens to collective judgment when institutions route their remembering through AI.

Institutions are beginning to shift memory and judgment to AI systems at organizational scale — retrieval instead of remembering, fluent synthesis instead of deliberation. The individual effects are studied; the institutional effects are not, even though most consequential decisions are made by institutions. This project asks the question in public, from inside digital culture: **what happens to a society when its organizations can retrieve everything and remember nothing?**

The project continues the *New Institutions* essay series (three essays published, including *Hiding in Plain Sight* on the difference between storage and memory) and develops it into a sustained public research agenda with three lines of inquiry:

1. **Retrieval versus memory.** Institutional memory is not storage; it is the capacity to make the past available to present judgment. What exactly is lost when AI summarization substitutes for it — and can the loss be made observable?
2. **Fluency versus judgment.** AI compresses weak institutional context into confident output. When does that fluency displace the deliberation it imitates?
3. **Cognitive monoculture.** When thousands of organizations route their sense-making through identical foundation models, do their judgments converge — and what does a monoculture of institutional cognition do to public life?

## Why this belongs in Digitale cultuur

This is research and reflection on internet culture and the digitally-mediated institution — the field definition's own terrain. It treats AI not as a tool to be adopted or refused but as a cultural force reorganizing how institutions remember, and it does so through the essay: a form digital culture has always used to think about itself. The work is anchored in Dutch institutional practice and published in the open.

## Deliverables (12 months)

- **Six new essays** in the *New Institutions* series, published openly at yeehaa.io and syndicated.
- **A flagship long-form publication**, *The Outsourced Institution*, combining the framework, case evidence from real deployments, and a practical diagnostic: "is this system extending our judgment or replacing it?"
- **Open-notebook research** in a public, provenance-tracked knowledge base, so every claim in the essays can be traced to its source — the method is itself a demonstration of the argument.
- **Two public events** (NL) presenting the work to the digital-culture field.

## Method

Practice-based inquiry. As a philosopher-builder who deploys knowledge infrastructure in organizations, I trace what concretely changes when an organization introduces AI into its knowledge work: which records become retrievable, which workflows reorganize, which outputs get treated as authoritative, who is still expected to remember the reasons. Transactive memory theory (Wegner) and distributed cognition (Hutchins) are the conceptual frame; the essays test the two working distinctions — storage vs. memory, fluency vs. judgment — against real episodes.
