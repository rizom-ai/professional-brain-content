## Statement (~250 words)

Cosmos asks for philosopher-builders. That is not a persona I adopted for this application; it is the actual shape of my career. I spent a decade in academic philosophy and media theory, left to build knowledge infrastructure, and now deploy AI agents inside organizations through my company, Rizom.

From that vantage point, the central threat AI poses to human autonomy is not dramatic misalignment but quiet substitution: institutions offloading memory and judgment to systems that compress weak context into confident, fluent output. Organizations that can retrieve everything and remember nothing. Assistants optimized for frictionless dependence rather than practical wisdom. The question I put to every system I build — *is this extending our judgment or replacing it?* — is, I think, the engineering form of Cosmos's own question about autonomy.

My answer in code is the *situated agent*: a named role, bounded scope, provenance-carrying output, explicit escalation, and a genuine contestation path — built as open-source infrastructure (github.com/rizom-ai/brains) and run in the open in a public, provenance-tracked notebook (yeehaa.io/dashboard). As a fellow I would develop this into two linked deliverables: a published design standard for accountable agents, and a study of what institutional offloading does to collective judgment, grounded in transactive memory theory and in the real deployments I run.

I build the alternative I argue for, and I argue in public with receipts.
