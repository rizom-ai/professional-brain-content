## The problem

What a hub actually sells is what happens between members: the introductions, the expertise in the room, the ideas an event leaves behind. Desks can be rented anywhere; the community is the product. Yet the knowledge of who knows what, who should meet whom, and what came out of last month's session sits in the community manager's head and a chat archive nobody reads. When they go on holiday, the intros stop. When they leave, years of accumulated context leave with them, and their successor starts from a member list and a shared drive.

## What a Community Brain is

A Community Brain is a knowledge system with an AI agent in front of it, running on infrastructure the hub controls. Your team — and, as far as you allow, your members — talk to it in the chat where the community already lives. It captures what flows past: event write-ups, member introductions, links, the questions people keep asking. Everything is stored as plain, portable files and becomes searchable by meaning, not just keywords. Ask it who in the community has worked with municipal grants, and it answers from what the community has actually said and done. **We deploy and run it; you own it.**

## What it does

- **Events that leave something behind.** Sessions, talks, and hallway conversations get captured while they're fresh, so the next event builds on the last one instead of starting over.
- **Members who find each other.** Anyone can look up who knows what across the whole community and spot the introductions worth making.
- **Onboarding with context.** New members land in the community's accumulated knowledge from day one instead of a welcome email, and routine questions stop landing on the community manager's plate. The role shifts from human search engine back to judgment, taste, and trust.
- **An institutional memory that survives turnover.** Staff changes stop being an amnesia event. What the community learned stays with the community.

## Why you can trust it with your community

The brain is AGPL open source and stores everything as plain, portable files on infrastructure you control. There is no platform between you and your community's memory, no per-seat pricing that punishes growth, and no exit fee: if you ever want to run it yourself, export everything and go. The offer only works if leaving is easy; that's deliberate.
