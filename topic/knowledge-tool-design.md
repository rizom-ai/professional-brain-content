---
title: 'Portable, Federated Knowledge Infrastructure'
---
Open-source, self-hosted infrastructure for producing, preserving, citing, and sharing knowledge while keeping data and governance under the control of its owners. It supports plain-text and machine-readable workflows, complete exports, importers and exporters, bidirectional synchronization, portable data models, citations, relationships, metadata, provenance, and round-trip conformance testing with transparent loss reports. Research notebooks and institutional repositories can expose source materials, revisions, working distinctions, and abandoned directions alongside finished conclusions, creating traceable and durable institutional memory rather than opaque summaries. Independently governed knowledge spaces may federate through selective, consent-based discovery, sharing, and citation without requiring dependence on a central platform. Core concerns include self-hosting, practical exit rights, archive preservation, semantic metadata, open licensing, privacy, human oversight, correction and reversal mechanisms, inspectable methods, extensible tools, and plural community governance. The domain applies across education, media, culture, health, government, and organizational settings, supporting collaborative learning, public-interest archives, digital autonomy, and durable knowledge commons while resisting vendor dependence and platform capture.
