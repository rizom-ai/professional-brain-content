---
title: Trustworthy Knowledge Infrastructure
---
Designing open, portable, interoperable, and user-controlled systems for creating, structuring, preserving, connecting, evaluating, retrieving, and applying knowledge. Trustworthy infrastructure makes data, claims, decisions, ownership, provenance, and automated actions inspectable, traceable, attributable, verifiable, and contestable. It treats documents, archives, context, relationships, semantic metadata, citations, lived practices, and institutional memory as interconnected resources. Core requirements include complete and machine-readable export of content, metadata, relationships, and provenance; round-trip verification; conformance testing; open specifications; open-source or AGPL-licensed tooling; self-hosted deployment; plugin architectures; transparent governance; cybersecurity and privacy; quality assessment; archive audits; documented evaluation; bounded authority; human oversight; and mechanisms for correction, reversal, escalation, and exit. A credible portability guarantee requires evidence that exported knowledge is complete, usable elsewhere, and not merely available through a nominal export function. Public documentation, deployment records, failure findings, ratings, and reviews strengthen accountability while preserving traceable responsibility. The domain applies to libraries, universities, museums, broadcasters, care institutions, research, education, public-interest organizations, professional settings, collaborative curation, organizational learning, AI agents, LLM workflows, and private knowledge systems. Its central principle is that genuine institutional ownership requires a testable ability to inspect, control, migrate, and exit from knowledge platforms.
