---
title: Knowledge Infrastructure and Data Practices
---
Designing open, portable, extensible, and user-owned systems for creating, structuring, preserving, connecting, evaluating, retrieving, and applying knowledge. These systems treat data, documents, archives, context, reasoning, relationships, semantic metadata, provenance, citations, lived practices, and institutional memory as interconnected resources rather than neutral representations of reality. The domain includes implementation-neutral data models, open-source and self-hosted platforms, plugin architectures, interoperable export and import, and round-trip verification to ensure that independent systems can faithfully reconstruct knowledge and its relationships. It also emphasizes data governance, quality assessment, recurring archive audits, accessibility, representation, agency, privacy, and human control. Applications span libraries, universities, museums, broadcasters, care institutions, research, education, public-interest organizations, and professional settings, including AI agents, LLM workflows, private organizational knowledge tools, collaborative curation, practitioner networks, participatory learning, and organizational decision-making. Effective infrastructure reveals gaps and contradictions, supports creative and interdisciplinary research, enables responsible AI-assisted analysis, and strengthens retrieval, navigation, collective intelligence, and organizational learning. Keywords: knowledge infrastructure, portable knowledge systems, data governance, interoperable data models, digital archives, institutional memory, provenance, semantic metadata, citation management, data quality, archive audits, round-trip verification, open-source platforms, self-hosted deployment, AI agents, LLM workflows, private organizational AI, collaborative curation, organizational learning
