---
title: Plural Digital Ecosystems
---
A federated network of personal and shared knowledge spaces in which independently operated agents retain local ownership while participating in collective learning. Each person can maintain a portable, self-hosted brain, while cohorts form shared brains that act as collective memory. Knowledge moves between spaces through consent rather than central control, supporting Offcourse’s crowdlearning model of collaborative curation instead of fixed courses. Key capabilities include discovery and federation between brains, public maps or indexed agents, constellations organized around shared substance, and group workflows for contributing, reviewing, and surfacing knowledge no individual holds alone. The absence of constellations may reflect either missing software or a cold-start participation problem, making ecosystem formation as important as technical development. Keywords: federated knowledge networks, self-hosted agents, personal knowledge spaces, collective memory, crowdlearning, consent-based sharing, agent discovery, knowledge constellations, group curation, local autonomy
