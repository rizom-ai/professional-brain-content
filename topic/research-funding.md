---
title: Research Funding
---
A systematic approach to identifying, evaluating, pursuing, and managing diversified funding for interdisciplinary research and build-oriented initiatives in institutional responsibility, AI governance, digital culture, open-source software, and shared infrastructure. It covers grants, fellowships, public programs, philanthropy, residencies, partnerships, and open-source funding, matching each opportunity to a clearly defined project scope—whether research-focused or infrastructure-focused—without duplicating related applications. Effective practice uses reusable proposal assets such as problem statements, methods, white papers, writing samples, videos, artifacts, and researcher profiles, adapting them to each funder’s priorities and format. Opportunities are assessed by eligibility, thematic fit, award size, deadlines, application status, project duration, co-financing, residency, partnership requirements, organizational vehicle, and administrative constraints. A funding pipeline should track near-term and long-term opportunities, including unsuitable or closed programs, to reduce repeated effort and improve future funder matching. Keywords: research grants, funding pipeline, interdisciplinary research, AI governance, digital culture, institutional responsibility, open-source infrastructure, proposal development, fellowships, philanthropy, public funding, funder eligibility, co-financing, research partnerships, grant tracking
