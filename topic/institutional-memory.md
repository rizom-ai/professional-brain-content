---
title: Institutional Memory
---
This topic examines how institutions preserve, retrieve, and use past experience when AI systems become embedded in knowledge work. It distinguishes institutional memory from storage: memory makes past decisions, reasons, disagreements, and context available for present judgment. The research investigates whether AI retrieval and summarization systems weaken deliberation by making information accessible while obscuring its significance, provenance, and rationale, potentially replacing rather than extending institutional judgment. Transactive memory and distributed cognition provide conceptual frameworks for analysing these changes, including the risk of cognitive monoculture when organizations rely on shared foundation models for sense-making. Practice-based inquiry, case evidence, and an open research notebook serve as methods and a counter-example by preserving revisions, sources, distinctions, and dead ends so that institutional reasoning remains traceable.

Keywords: institutional memory, AI-mediated knowledge work, organizational judgment, information retrieval, AI summarization, deliberation, decision rationale, distributed cognition, transactive memory, cognitive monoculture, research traceability
