---
title: Situated AI
---
Situated AI concerns the design and deployment of AI agents embedded in specific organizational, social, and cultural contexts rather than treated as standalone predictive services. Such systems participate in institutional coordination, workplace practices, distributed networks, and collective knowledge processes while extending human judgment instead of quietly replacing it. They require defined roles, bounded authority, context-aware access, predictable behavior, provenance-carrying outputs, traceable actions, and explicit escalation, review, and contestation paths connected to identifiable human or institutional authority. The approach addresses responsibility laundering, fragmented organizational memory, and the gradual substitution of institutional judgment by emphasizing human autonomy, accountability, and collective evaluation. It includes virtual team members, workflow automation, modular agents with interchangeable capabilities, open-source infrastructure, interoperability, shared organizational memory, knowledge provenance, reference implementations, and real-world human-AI collaboration. Its intellectual foundations span philosophy, cultural analysis, humanities computing, and software architecture, with institutional evaluation used to assess how agents affect collective behavior and organizational judgment.

Keywords: embedded AI, institutional context, social computing, organizational workflows, virtual team members, modular agents, distributed networks, human-AI collaboration, organizational memory, knowledge infrastructure, agent accountability, bounded authority, provenance, interoperability, escalation rules, contestability, human autonomy, collective judgment, open-source infrastructure
