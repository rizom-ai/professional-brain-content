---
title: Situated AI
---
Situated AI examines the design, deployment, and governance of AI systems embedded in organizational, social, cultural, technical, and institutional contexts rather than treated as standalone automation. It connects infrastructure building, philosophical inquiry, and field observation to study how AI reshapes records, workflows, authority, memory, deliberation, and public life. The domain includes interoperable agents, portable knowledge, organizational memory, distributed coordination, workflow support, retrieval-augmented generation, shared lexicons, and open standards such as the Model Context Protocol and AT Protocol. It also addresses risks of cognitive monoculture, in which reliance on identical foundation models can converge institutional judgment and substitute automated outputs for human or organizational reasoning. Reliable and autonomous systems require local adaptation, explicit roles, bounded authority, provenance, traceability, predictable behavior, escalation and contestation procedures, human oversight, and clear responsibility. Emphasis is placed on open, governed, sustainable infrastructure that supports portability, institutional sovereignty, public technical learning, and human judgment beyond individual products or funding cycles.
