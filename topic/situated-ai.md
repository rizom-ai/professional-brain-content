---
title: Situated AI
---
Situated AI Systems examines how human-AI systems are designed, deployed, and governed within the institutional, organizational, technical, and social contexts where they operate. It covers LLM workflows, autonomous agents, virtual team members, organizational memory, and production-ready knowledge infrastructure that helps systems access, organize, and reuse institutional knowledge. The core concern is explicit role and system design: defining authority, access, limitations, responsibility, and reliable action so AI functions as an accountable collaborator rather than merely optimizing workflows. Documented architectural patterns make behavior more understandable, repeatable, and reviewable, while independent review is especially important for high-stakes decisions. Responsible deployment must also account for how AI can amplify unclear roles, broken archives, unresolved conflicts, weak accountability, and politically situated knowledge that fluent outputs may present as objective. The topic connects technical architecture with product development, operational responsibility, and the sustained design of AI systems for real social and organizational settings. Keywords: artificial intelligence, human-AI systems, large language models, autonomous agents, organizational memory, knowledge infrastructure, system architecture, role design, AI governance, accountability, institutional context, independent review, production deployment
