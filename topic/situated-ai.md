---
title: Situated AI
---
Situated AI examines how AI agents and human-AI systems are designed, deployed, and governed within the institutional, organizational, technical, and social contexts where they operate. It emphasizes that effective AI collaboration requires more than generic intelligence or broad data access: systems must account for situated knowledge, human agency, domain practices, operational constraints, and the roles and authority of participants. The topic covers large language model workflows, autonomous and modular agents, virtual team members, organizational knowledge coordination, and production-ready knowledge infrastructure. Key design concerns include capabilities, access, limitations, interaction patterns, responsibility, and readiness for testing or network expansion. Architectural patterns and organizational memory can make behavior more understandable, repeatable, and reviewable, while independent oversight remains important for high-stakes decisions. Situated deployment must also address how AI can amplify unclear roles, fragmented archives, unresolved conflicts, weak accountability, and politically situated knowledge that fluent outputs may present as objective. Keywords: artificial intelligence, human-AI systems, large language models, autonomous agents, modular agents, virtual team members, situated knowledge, organizational memory, knowledge coordination, knowledge infrastructure, system architecture, role design, human agency, collaborative AI, AI governance, accountability, institutional context, independent review, production deployment
