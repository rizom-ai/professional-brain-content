---
title: Digital Trust
---
Digital trust is the foundation for digital systems and AI agents whose claims, decisions, and actions can be understood, traced, verified, attributed, and challenged. It depends on inspectable infrastructure rather than fluent answers or institutional assurances alone: provenance records identify the sources, models, agents, and processes behind outputs; defined roles and bounded authority clarify permitted actions; transparent data practices, cybersecurity, privacy, and accountable governance support reliable operation; and documented deployment and evaluation practices provide evidence of performance. Escalation procedures preserve meaningful human involvement when situations exceed an agent’s scope, while contestation mechanisms give affected people a practical route to question, correct, or reverse harmful, unjustified, or erroneous decisions. Public documentation, open research, references, project records, ratings, reviews, and other credibility signals can strengthen trust when they are supported by verifiable evidence, clear responsibility, and traceable decisions—not opaque automation or unsupported claims. Digital trust applies across digital platforms, professional services, education, research, technology partnerships, and organizational AI systems, especially where automated decisions have consequential effects.
