## What this project is

No field invests more in memory than the art field. Works are preserved, catalogued, insured, climate-controlled — yet the knowledge behind the work is the first thing to disappear: why a show was made, how an artist was found, what a program taught. A curator leaves, and the reasoning behind a decade of programming leaves with them. A residency ends, and the next cohort starts from zero.

**The archive keeps the works. It loses the context.**

This project builds the missing layer: a **living archive** for the art field — a knowledge environment in which the context is kept alongside the works. Decisions with their rationale. Programs with their thinking. Relationships with their history. Open source, plain text, self-hosted, portable — built on infrastructure the maker already builds and operates, shaped here for what the art field actually holds.

One maker builds it, and runs the first one **in public** for twelve months. The public/private contradiction that sinks this idea in other sectors does not exist here: exhibition histories, catalogues, programs and curatorial statements are already public genres. The context this project keeps alive is material that *belongs* in public and currently dies in private. The forgetting is not an institution's dirty laundry — it is the field's own commons failing.

## Deliverables (12 months)

1. **The instrument** — the living-archive environment, open source (AGPL), shaped for the art field: works, programs, decisions-with-rationale, funding histories, relationships. Portable and self-hostable by any institution, studio or artist afterwards.
2. **The first living archive, live and public from early in the project** — built from the field's public record, growing all year. ⚠ **Which corpus/scene it starts from is Jan Hein's decision, needed before filing** — deliberately not filled in here.
3. **Published accounts** of what keeping the context reveals — continuing the *New Institutions* series (number: Jan Hein's call; the archive is the primary deliverable, the writing is its yield).
4. **One public working session** in the Netherlands, second half of the project, where the field engages with the archive directly.

Explicitly out of scope: the book (*The Outsourced Institution* gets written on top of this later, unfunded here), formal institutional partnerships (solo band), and any protocol/federation work (that is NLnet's).

## Why this belongs in Digitale cultuur

The art field's memory is the fund's own terrain — erfgoed-adjacent, and squarely inside internetcultuur and art-science in the art. 1.22 field definition. The project treats the archive not as storage but as a cultural form to be redesigned, and does it by building.

The maker's history in this field is not decorative: research project at the Rijksmuseum (*Augmenting Masterpieces*, Best of the Web nomination 2015), research fellow at the Jan van Eyck Academie, a decade teaching cultural analysis, and Public Badges deployed across Dutch cultural institutions via PublicSpaces.

Everything produced is openly licensed and publicly accessible throughout — art. 6.1.k as a condition, and also the point.

## Method

Practice-based making. The infrastructure exists (the maker's own knowledge-environment stack, running publicly at yeehaa.io for over a year); the work is shaping it to the art field's material and proving it on a real public corpus, in the open. Transactive memory theory (Wegner) and distributed cognition (Hutchins) frame what "context kept alongside works" concretely means: not more storage, but the capacity to make the past available to present judgment.
